# CineFX Narrative Mega Events

These four presets are written as actual live-event stories first, then implemented as timelines. The goal is not to keep particles active for five minutes. Every 15–30 seconds the audience should receive a new piece of information, a visual reveal, a movement beat, a threat, a reversal or a payoff.

All four events are `terrain_adaptive`: the showcase command chooses a nearby stable surface instead of blindly using the command source Y. Ground actors and structures are designed around the local surface while airborne story objects remain absolute.

## 1. Zero Point: The Broken Protocol — ~6:00

### Premise
A research team has found a dormant reality core below the area. Four stabilizer pylons are deployed to lift it safely. A Rift Sentinel appears and corrupts the protocol because it wants to use the core to open a permanent bridge between realities.

### Beat sheet
- **0:00–0:25 — Survey.** Calm research zone. Engineer villagers walk between four marked stabilizer sites. Chrono drones scan the ground. A short establishing camera introduces the area.
- **0:25–0:50 — First pulse.** The ground answers the scan. A circular energy pulse crosses the terrain. The buried core becomes visible below the surface.
- **0:50–1:20 — Stabilization.** Four physical stabilizer pylons arrive/deploy one after another. Each locks on with a beam to the core. The core rises gradually; its light strengthens as every pylon comes online.
- **1:20–1:45 — Something is wrong.** The drones stop their normal orbit and converge. The sky color changes. Small rifts appear around the perimeter, initially harmless.
- **1:45–2:20 — Sentinel arrival.** A premium Sentinel emerges from the largest rift. It walks toward the nearest pylon instead of standing still. Engineers flee along different routes. The Sentinel corrupts pylon A, then B; their beams change color and the core begins to destabilize.
- **2:20–3:00 — Reality leak.** Short pieces of four realities appear around the event: ice spires, void crystals, desert debris and neon geometry. They appear in separate sectors so the player can understand that the core is pulling other worlds in.
- **3:00–3:35 — Countermeasure.** Remaining drones form a ring and fire synchronized beams into the core. The Sentinel moves to the core and attempts to absorb it. A cinematic camera lasts only for the actual confrontation.
- **3:35–4:15 — Core escape.** The core tears free of the stabilizers, rises and starts orbiting above the battlefield. The ground fissure grows under it. Portal fragments rotate around the core.
- **4:15–4:55 — Collapse.** Stabilizers explode sequentially rather than all at once. Their destruction causes increasingly strong shocks. The Sentinel is hit by the final discharge and is thrown back into its rift.
- **4:55–5:25 — Zero Point fracture.** The core fractures into thousands of pieces and time appears to slow. Particles reverse toward the center, then freeze for a short silence beat.
- **5:25–6:00 — Rewind and repair.** Shards fly back together, the fissure closes visually, foreign-reality objects dissolve and the core descends into a stable containment cradle. Engineers cautiously return. Final message: `PROTOCOL RESTORED`.

## 2. Starfall: Last Evacuation — ~5:30

### Premise
A civilian settlement detects a fleet crossing the sky. The first ships are scouts, but their purpose is to place beacons for a mothership. The evacuation becomes a defense operation when the mothership starts charging a surface-cleansing beam.

### Beat sheet
- **0:00–0:25 — False calm.** Civilians move normally, defense drones patrol and distant fighter silhouettes cross the horizon.
- **0:25–0:55 — Detection.** Three Starfall fighters perform a low pass at different heights. Alarm lighting starts. Civilians switch from wander to evacuation routes.
- **0:55–1:30 — Beacon drop.** Fighters place three rift beacons around the area. Pillager-style raiders emerge from them and march toward the center.
- **1:30–2:00 — Mothership reveal.** The sky darkens and the mothership crosses above the area. A short camera move reveals scale, then control returns.
- **2:00–2:35 — Defense grid.** Stabilizer models are repurposed as defense pylons. They deploy around the settlement and fire at passing fighters. Fighters use actual spline paths, not stationary meshes.
- **2:35–3:10 — Shield failure.** A fighter crashes into one defense node. The environment cracks around the impact. The mothership descends closer and its engines illuminate the ground.
- **3:10–3:45 — Boarding wave.** More raiders arrive while civilians reach the safe side of the arena. Defense drones move between targets. The mothership beam begins charging visibly.
- **3:45–4:20 — Beam firing.** The mothership fires. A moving impact line and expanding shockwaves cross the event space; the environment responds with cracks and debris. Camera is locked only during the strongest 15–20 second beat.
- **4:20–4:50 — Counterattack.** Surviving fighters turn against the mothership after one is captured by the defense system. Three fighter paths converge on separate engines.
- **4:50–5:15 — Chain destruction.** Engines fail one after another, the mothership rolls, loses altitude and fractures progressively instead of disappearing instantly.
- **5:15–5:30 — Aftermath.** Remaining rifts collapse, raiders vanish/flee, emergency lighting fades and civilians begin returning. Final message: `EVACUATION CANCELLED // SKY CLEAR`.

## 3. Titan: Heart of the Mountain — ~5:50

### Premise
An excavation team activates a buried machine believing it is a power source. It is actually the heart of a colossal ancient guardian. The Titan wakes to seal a sky rift, but the research team initially mistakes it for the threat.

### Beat sheet
- **0:00–0:30 — Excavation.** Workers, scanning drones and a visible exposed heart crystal establish the objective. Small machinery lights pulse in sequence.
- **0:30–1:00 — Activation.** The heart answers the scan. Stone plates rise from the ground around the excavation and the terrain starts to crack.
- **1:00–1:35 — Awakening.** The Titan rises in stages: head/torso silhouette, then full body. Its first movement is slow and heavy; footsteps generate localized dust and shock rings.
- **1:35–2:05 — Panic.** Workers flee. Defense pylons deploy and attack the Titan. The Titan walks toward the center and turns toward each source of damage.
- **2:05–2:40 — Misunderstanding.** A large rift opens behind the players. The Titan stops attacking the defense grid and turns toward the rift. The heart light changes from orange to cyan.
- **2:40–3:15 — Rift creature.** A Sentinel and flying shards emerge. The Titan physically moves toward them. Drones retarget from Titan to rift.
- **3:15–3:55 — Battle.** Titan beam, rift beam, particle forces and debris alternate rather than happening continuously. Several short impact beats make the fight readable.
- **3:55–4:30 — Heart exposed.** The Titan is damaged and its heart becomes visible as a separate model attached near its chest. The rift starts pulling the heart outward.
- **4:30–5:05 — Seal.** Defense pylons change allegiance and stabilize the Titan heart. The Titan uses the stored energy to close the rift. The rift collapses inward with reverse particles.
- **5:05–5:35 — Price.** Closing the rift petrifies the Titan from the feet upward. It takes several last steps to move away from the settlement before collapsing.
- **5:35–5:50 — Memorial.** Dust settles. The heart remains faintly lit inside the petrified remains. Workers return and place four small lights around it. Final message: `GUARDIAN STATUS // DORMANT`.

## 4. Chrono Nexus: One Minute Too Many — ~6:30

### Premise
A Chrono drone is demonstrating a harmless one-minute rewind. The experiment succeeds, but the reset happens a second time by itself. Every loop brings back pieces of a reality that should no longer exist.

### Beat sheet
- **0:00–0:30 — Demonstration.** A Chrono drone circles a small beacon while technicians walk normally. A countdown indicates the planned rewind.
- **0:30–1:00 — First rewind.** A small structure breaks and immediately reconstructs. Particles reverse. Everyone resumes exactly as before: success.
- **1:00–1:25 — Uncommanded reset.** The same sound and visual cue happen again without the technicians triggering it. NPC paths rewind and replay out of sync.
- **1:25–2:00 — Void echo.** One sector of the environment becomes a void version. A duplicate Chrono drone appears and follows the original with a delayed path.
- **2:00–2:35 — Frozen echo.** A second loop overlays ice geometry. Two versions of several moving actors are now visible at different timeline offsets.
- **2:35–3:10 — Desert echo.** Sand/debris and warm lighting overwrite another sector. The main portal begins showing alternating colors faster and faster.
- **3:10–3:45 — Neon future.** The fourth reality appears as neon structures and drones. All four reality sectors now coexist.
- **3:45–4:20 — Timeline collision.** Objects from different loops start moving through the same space. The Chrono drones form a synchronized orbit to stop the process.
- **4:20–4:55 — Failed merge.** The merge creates a huge central portal and briefly pulls actors/particles toward it. Environment fragments fracture into suspended pieces.
- **4:55–5:35 — Manual rewind.** The drones reverse direction. Events replay backward in compressed form: neon disappears, desert disappears, ice disappears, void disappears.
- **5:35–6:05 — Original minute restored.** The world returns to the opening state, but one extra drone remains. It slowly realizes it does not belong and flies into the closing portal.
- **6:05–6:30 — Clean timeline.** The portal closes, the countdown returns to zero and the technicians resume their original routes. Final message: `TIMELINE 01 // STABLE`.

## Rhythm rules used by all narrative presets

- Never leave more than ~30 seconds without a new readable beat.
- Camera locks are short and reserved for reveals or payoffs; normal spectacle happens with player control.
- Large effects have anticipation, action and aftermath. Do not start every system simultaneously.
- NPC movement communicates intent: working, evacuating, attacking, regrouping and returning.
- Models have narrative roles and reappear across beats so the viewer understands what matters.
- Environment changes are grouped into sectors/causes rather than random decorative growth.
- Every final destruction has a visible consequence and a cleanup/resolution phase.
